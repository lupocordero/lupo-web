Sóller and Deià are, without a doubt, the most requested combination for elopements in Mallorca, and for good reason: in under 15 minutes by car you can go from a valley of orange groves to a stone village perched above the sea. After guiding many couples through this area as an [elopement photographer in Mallorca](/en/elopement), here's a complete guide to help you plan yours.

This guide covers everything I usually walk couples through in our first planning call — why this part of the island works so well for an elopement, the specific locations worth considering, when to go for the best light, and the practical details around permits, footwear and timing that make the difference between a smooth day and a stressful one.

## Why choose Sóller and Deià for an elopement

The main reason is the sheer variety of scenery within such a small radius: mountains, orange groves, stone villages, coves and viewpoints over the Mediterranean, all reachable without long drives. Both areas also have a relaxed, authentic atmosphere, far from the more touristy parts of the south coast — something many couples especially value for such an intimate moment.

## The best locations

### Orange grove paths, Sóller

Ideal for the couple's session — filtered light, a calm atmosphere, and in spring the scent of orange blossom makes the whole experience even more special. A great choice for early morning.

### Plaça de la Constitució, Sóller

If you want a village feel with Modernist architecture, Sóller's central square offers a beautiful backdrop, though it's worth going early to avoid the bustle of tourists and the famous tram.

### The cemetery viewpoint, Deià

With panoramic views over the sea and the village, this is one of the most photogenic corners of the island — perfect for a short symbolic ceremony at sunset. It's a few minutes' walk from the village center.

### Cala de Deià

For couples who don't mind a bit of a walk (the descent is fairly steep), Cala de Deià offers a wild, beautiful setting with cliffs and crystal-clear water. Best in the morning, when there are fewer people and the light falls directly into the cove.

### Mountain viewpoints toward Puig Major

For those after something more dramatic and less frequented, the mountain roads between Sóller and Deià have several viewpoints with spectacular views over the whole valley — ideal if total privacy is what you're after.

### A quieter alternative: Fornalutx

Just a few minutes from Sóller, the tiny village of Fornalutx is often overlooked in favor of its larger neighbor, but it's arguably even more picturesque — narrow stone streets, terraced hillsides and almost no traffic. If you want a village backdrop with fewer visitors than central Sóller, it's worth the short detour.

## When to go: the best light and time of year

For both areas, I almost always recommend the first two hours after sunrise or the last two before sunset. Midday summer light is harsh and creates unflattering shadows, especially in a setting this bright.

As for the time of year: March and April bring orange blossom and pleasant temperatures; May and June have long days with soft light lasting well into the evening; September and October keep good weather with fewer tourists than peak summer. July and August are possible too, but need careful timing to avoid the heat and crowds.

Even winter has its appeal here — December through February bring crisp, clear light and near-total privacy at most of these locations, along with citrus trees heavy with fruit rather than blossom. It's colder, especially at sunrise, but for couples who don't mind wrapping up warm, it's a genuinely underrated season for this part of the island.

## Logistics and permits

For purely photographic sessions on public trails, beaches and viewpoints, a special permit usually isn't needed. If your elopement includes a legally binding ceremony, you'll need to coordinate with an authorized officiant — I can recommend trusted contacts who work regularly in the area. For private estates or specific locations with restricted access, I handle the necessary permit in advance as part of planning the session.

## What to bring

Comfortable footwear is essential — several of the best locations in Deià involve walking on uneven stone paths. For brides in long dresses, I also recommend a second pair of easy-to-walk-in shoes for moving between locations. Bring water, especially during the warmer months, and a light layer if you're staying for sunset, since the temperature drops quickly in the mountains once the sun goes down.

If you're planning a symbolic ceremony, think about small details in advance too — vows printed or written out so nerves don't get in the way, a small speaker if you want music, and any rings, flowers or décor items brought with you, since options for last-minute purchases are limited in both villages.
